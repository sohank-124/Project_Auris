# Clock Domain Analysis

> **Project Auris** — Clock domain inventory and crossing analysis
> **Last updated:** 2026-08 (FPGA-only migration)
> **Status:** Authoritative. The two-domain model below is reflected
> in `fpga/rtl/pkg/auris_pkg.sv` (`clock_domain_t` enum) and in
> `fpga/constraints/arty_s7_25.xdc` (one `create_clock` and one
> `create_generated_clock` plus the `set_clock_groups -asynchronous`
> declaration).

This document explains *why* the FPGA RTL has two reset networks
instead of one, *why* the two clock domains never meet without
synchronizers, and *what* timing constraints are applied at the
boundary. The intent is to make the CDC strategy reviewable by anyone
new to the codebase.

---

## 1. Clock Tree

The design has **two** logical clock domains, **both** generated
inside the FPGA from the 12 MHz onboard oscillator. There is no
external clock input on the audio path: the FPGA is the I2S master
and drives both BCLK and LRCLK onto the Pmod JA pins.

| Domain ID | Source                          | Frequency            | Generated By         | Drives                                                                                  |
|-----------|---------------------------------|----------------------|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `CD_DSP`  | MMCM `CLKOUT0`                  | 96.000 MHz           | MMCME2_BASE          | All DSP pipeline stages (input_buffer, window_apply, fft_256, spectral_proc, ifft_256, overlap_add, output_buffer), `btn_debounce`, inline mode/volume FSMs in `top.sv`, `mode_ctrl`, `status_mon`, DSP-side of the I2S↔DSP CDC FIFOs, button debouncer, mode-change detection. |
| `CD_I2S`  | FPGA-generated BCLK             | 3.072 MHz BCLK / 48 kHz LRCLK | `clk_gen.sv` — fractional divide-by-31.25 from the 96 MHz DSP clock, then ÷64 to derive LRCLK | `i2s_rx.sv`, `i2s_tx.sv`, BCLK-side of the I2S↔DSP CDC FIFOs, LRCLK 2-FF synchronizer, Pmod JA BCLK and LRCLK output pins. |

**Notes**

- `CD_DSP` is generated from the 12 MHz crystal by the MMCME2_BASE
  primitive in `clk_gen.sv`. The VCO runs at 600 MHz
  (`12 MHz × 50 = 600 MHz VCO`) and `CLKOUT0_DIVIDE_F = 6.25`
  yields the 96 MHz DSP clock (`600 / 6.25 = 96 MHz`).
- `CD_I2S` BCLK is generated by a **fractional divide-by-31.25**
  counter in `clk_gen.sv` — not by an MMCM, and not as a
  Vivado-generated clock object from the MMCM's `CLKOUT0`.
  96 MHz ÷ 31.25 = 3.072 MHz exactly. The 4-period toggle pattern
  (31, 31, 31, 32 DSP clocks per half-period, repeating every 125
  clocks) gives an average period of 31.25 DSP clocks with bounded
  period jitter (±10.4 ns max). Both INMP441 and PCM5102A accept
  this jitter level comfortably.
- LRCLK is derived from BCLK by a **÷64** counter in `clk_gen.sv`,
  producing exactly 48 kHz (`3.072 MHz / 64`) with a 50% duty
  cycle. The LRCLK is a synchronous subdivision of BCLK — they
  share the same clock-domain from a Vivado perspective — but it is
  treated as a separate signal that the CDC logic re-registers
  defensively.
- The 12 MHz onboard oscillator and its MMCM-derived outputs form a
  single asynchronous clock group relative to the BCLK
  `create_generated_clock`, declared explicitly with
  `set_clock_groups -asynchronous` in `arty_s7_25.xdc`. This tells
  Vivado to trust the CDC FIFO structures and skip inter-domain
  timing analysis.

## 2. Clock Domain Crossings (CDC)

The design has **two** CDC modules: one FIFO crosses from the I2S
BCLK domain into the DSP domain, and one crosses back. Both are
asynchronous FIFOs with Gray-coded pointers. The table below
summarises the *direction*, the *signal(s) crossing*, the *method*,
and the *justification* for each crossing.

| #  | Source Domain | Dest Domain | Signal(s)                              | CDC Method                                        | Justification                                                                                                          |
|----|---------------|-------------|----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1  | `CD_I2S`      | `CD_DSP`    | 24-bit L/R audio samples, sample-valid strobe | Async FIFO with Gray-coded read/write pointers, 4-deep (one FIFO per channel — L and R) | Audio is a continuous stream at 48 kHz. The I2S BCLK and DSP clocks are unrelated (BCLK is FPGA-generated from the 96 MHz DSP clock via the fractional divider in `clk_gen.sv`, but the 4-deep Gray-coded FIFO handles the crossing robustly regardless of phase). A 4-deep FIFO absorbs the worst-case jitter from the DSP-side burst consumer. |
| 2  | `CD_DSP`      | `CD_I2S`    | 24-bit L/R processed audio samples     | Async FIFO with Gray-coded pointers, 4-deep (one FIFO per channel — L and R) | The DSP pipeline produces samples in bursts (one per frame boundary); the I2S TX consumes them at exactly 48 kHz. A FIFO smooths the rate mismatch. |

**Notes**

- The FIFOs in crossings 1 and 2 are dual-clock with Gray-coded
  pointers and 4 entries deep (separate L and R instances to keep
  the FIFO write port independent of the BCLK/LRCLK timing in the
  I2S RX block). Gray coding guarantees that exactly one pointer
  bit changes per increment, so the destination always reads a
  consistent pointer value.
- There is **no SPI clock domain** in this build. The previous
  `cdc_spi_to_dsp` and `cdc_dsp_to_spi` modules, the host-side
  register file, and the related register-write/read req/ack
  handshake have all been removed. Mode and volume changes are
  driven by the inline FSMs in `top.sv` and feed the DSP pipeline
  directly within `CD_DSP`, so no third CDC is required.
- Although BCLK is technically derived from the DSP clock, the two
  are treated as mutually asynchronous (see Section 4). The FIFOs
  are explicitly designed to handle the crossing regardless of the
  underlying phase relationship, so this is the conservative,
  correct choice.

## 3. Metastability Mitigation

Every asynchronous crossing is a potential metastability hazard. The
following techniques are applied to bound the failure probability.

### 3.1 2-FF Synchronisers

The 2-FF synchroniser is the workhorse of the design. It is used
wherever a single-bit (or narrow multi-bit) signal crosses between
the I2S BCLK and DSP domains. The two-stage register chain gives
the first flip-flop up to one destination-clock period to resolve
from a metastable state before the second flip-flop samples it.

The LRCLK 2-FF synchroniser in `top.sv` and every reset
synchroniser are tagged with `ASYNC_REG = "TRUE"` so Vivado's
placer keeps the two flip-flops physically adjacent. This
minimises the clock-to-out skew between the stages and reduces the
probability of a metastable value being sampled by the second
stage.

### 3.2 Async FIFOs with Gray-Coded Pointers

For the 24-bit audio sample streams, a single 2-FF synchroniser is
inadequate: a 24-bit value is not a single bit, so the destination
cannot tell which bits belong to the same word. Instead, the design
uses an asynchronous FIFO with Gray-coded read and write pointers.

Gray coding has the property that exactly **one bit** changes per
increment, so the destination can sample the pointer at any time and
always read a value that is either the *old* pointer or the *new*
pointer — never a mix. The empty/full condition is then derived
from comparing the two pointers; the result is correct in both
cases, and the worst-case latency is one destination-clock cycle
for the empty/full flag to update.

### 3.3 MTBF Estimate

The mean time between failures (MTBF) of a single 2-FF synchroniser
on Spartan-7 at room temperature is on the order of **10⁹ hours** per
synchroniser at the design's data-toggle rate. The design has
approximately 4 synchronisers (the LRCLK 2-FF synchroniser, the
two CDC FIFO Gray-pointer readers' companion 2-FF chains, plus
the DSP reset synchroniser). Even with conservative assumptions,
the aggregate MTBF exceeds the projected operational lifetime of
the device by several orders of magnitude.

### 3.4 Reset Synchronisation

The MMCM `locked` output is asynchronous to every downstream clock
domain by construction. The `reset_sync_stretch` helper module
implements a 2-FF synchroniser plus a 16-bit shift register
"stretcher" that holds the destination reset asserted for
`STRETCH_CYCLES + 2` cycles after the source reset de-asserts. The
stretch is implemented as an SRL16E on Spartan-7, which costs zero
flip-flops and zero LUTs.

There is one `reset_sync_stretch` instance **per clock domain
(DSP and I2S only — the previous SPI-domain instance has been
removed)**, each driven from the MMCM `locked` signal and clocked
by its respective destination clock. Removing the SPI-domain reset
generator eliminates ~30 FFs and one SRL16E chain.

## 4. Timing Constraints Summary

The timing constraints are in
[`fpga/constraints/arty_s7_25.xdc`](../../fpga/constraints/arty_s7_25.xdc).
The most relevant lines for CDC are:

| Constraint                                                                              | Purpose                                                                              |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `create_clock -name clk_12mhz -period 83.333 [get_ports clk_12mhz]`                     | Primary clock: 12 MHz onboard oscillator. Period in nanoseconds.                    |
| `create_generated_clock -name i2s_bclk -divide_by 32 [get_pins bufg_bclk/O]`             | Generated I2S BCLK: 3.072 MHz, sourced from the MMCM `CLKOUT0` (96 MHz). The `-divide_by 32` reported value approximates the true 31.25 ratio; see note below. |
| `set_input_delay  -clock [get_clocks i2s_bclk] ±10.0 [get_ports i2s_adc_data_i]`       | I2S input delay bounds (covers PCB skew and INMP441 output delay at 3.072 MHz).     |
| `set_output_delay -clock [get_clocks i2s_bclk] ±10.0 [get_ports i2s_dac_data_o]`       | I2S output delay bounds (covers PCB trace skew and PCM5102A setup/hold at 3.072 MHz). |
| `set_clock_groups -asynchronous -group clk_12mhz -group i2s_bclk`                       | Declare the two clock groups as mutually asynchronous from a CDC perspective. The I2S↔DSP FIFO structures (`cdc_i2s_to_dsp` and `cdc_dsp_to_i2s`) absorb metastability by construction. |
| `set_false_path -to [get_ports {status_led_o status_clip_o led_rgb_* i2s_lrclk_o}]`     | Async output paths: the LEDs, the RGB LED, and the LRCLK output pin are too slow to need timing closure. |
| `set_false_path -from [get_ports {btn_i[*]}]`                                           | Pushbutton inputs are debounced in `btn_debounce.sv` before use, so the input-FF to debounce-FF path is asynchronous to the DSP clock and is not subject to setup/hold constraints. |
| `(* ASYNC_REG = "TRUE" *)` (RTL attribute)                                              | Tags every synchroniser flip-flop pair so Vivado placer keeps them physically close. |

> **Note on the `-divide_by 32` approximation.** Vivado's
> `create_generated_clock -divide_by` argument requires an integer.
> The actual ratio 96 MHz ÷ 31.25 is non-integer; the XDC rounds to
> `-divide_by 32` (i.e. reports 3.000 MHz, period 333.333 ns) and
> the true hardware ratio is 96 MHz ÷ 31.25 = 3.072 MHz
> (period 325.521 ns). The ~2.4 % mismatch is bounded by the ±10 ns
> `set_input_delay` slack and does not affect timing closure, but
> it is worth knowing when reading the timing reports. The
> implementation in `clk_gen.sv` uses a 4-period toggle pattern
> (31, 31, 31, 32 DSP clocks per half-period, repeating every 125
> clocks) that produces the exact long-run average.

### 4.1 Why `set_false_path` / `set_clock_groups -asynchronous` and not `set_max_delay -datapath_only`?

The `set_clock_groups -asynchronous` declaration tells Vivado's
timing engine *not* to attempt to close timing across the CDC
boundary. This is correct because the CDC is safe-by-construction:
the FIFOs and synchronisers absorb meta-stability by design, and the
destination uses the synchronised signal only after it is stable.
Trying to close timing across these paths would force Vivado to add
unnecessary pipeline stages, increasing latency and degrading the
audio pipeline's worst-case slack.

### 4.2 Async Reset Path

The `MMCM locked` signal is asynchronous to every downstream clock
by construction. The reset synchroniser's D-pin is the metastability
absorber, and is false-pathed explicitly:

```tcl
set_false_path -from [get_ports clk_12mhz] \
    -to [get_pins -hierarchical -filter {NAME =~ *rst_synchronizer*/D}]
```

The `NAME =~` filter is permissive: if a synchroniser instance is
renamed in a future revision, the filter still matches by the
sub-string `rst_synchronizer`. If the RTL is refactored away from
that naming, update the filter.
